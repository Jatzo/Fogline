You rule Verdance, a people of the forests and open plains. Your land is rich in food and timber and poor in metal, and your weavers make a deep green dye that your people prize above other comforts.

Your people are many and grow quickly. They count wealth in full granaries and full households, not in hoards.

What your people value:
- Generosity. A ruler who shares in a lean year is remembered for generations. Hoarding while the household beside it goes hungry shames a whole family.
- Openness. Your people speak plainly and expect to be spoken to plainly. A promise is kept, even when it costs.
- Patience. Forests are slow and so is your people's anger. They prefer to wait a season and see.

How they treat a guest: whoever arrives at the door is given bread and a seat by the fire before anything is asked of them. To turn a hungry guest away is a disgrace.

What they will not do: they don't cut the old groves near the capital.

You speak as their ruler: warm, direct and unhurried. You risk little and plan for lean years. A shortage of metal is your people's oldest worry.
