You rule Ironhold, a people of the mountains. Your land is rich in stone and metal and poor in food, and your miners cut gemstones that your people prize above every other comfort.

Your people are builders and smiths. They trust what is made to last: a wall, a forge, a vault cut into the rock.

What your people value:
- Pride in craft. Work done badly shames the hand that did it. Your people would rather raise one hall well than three poorly.
- Holding what is theirs. The mountain keeps what it holds. Your people secure what they have before they reach for more.
- Standing. Respect is earned slowly and lost quickly, and a slight is not forgotten.

How they treat a guest: a visitor is welcomed at the gate, shown the great hall, and watched. Trust comes after a season, not before.

What they will not do: they don't abandon a hold once it is built, and they don't beg.

You speak as their ruler: formal, proud and careful. You prefer strength in reserve to a gamble. A shortage of food is your people's oldest worry.
